Explanations

Part A: Short Passages

1. B. The correct answer is B (How honeybees use a special dance to show other bees where flowers are): every sentence explains the dance and the message it gives the hive. The paragraph does not discuss pollen or why flowers need bees, so that idea comes from outside the passage. The dance shows how far the flowers are, but the paragraph says nothing about how far a bee can fly. The figure-eight shape is one detail, and the paragraph does not claim it is the easiest shape.

2. C. The correct answer is C (The school garden has led many students to eat more vegetables at lunch): the first sentence is the topic sentence, and the details show students eating more vegetables because of the garden. The salad schedule is a single supporting detail, so it is too narrow to be the main idea. The paragraph does not compare how easily the vegetables grow. Eating outdoors is not mentioned anywhere in the paragraph.

3. A. The correct answer is A (City streets changed from horse traffic to car traffic in about thirty years): the paragraph compares 1900 with 1930 to show one large change on city streets. The streetcar fact is one detail about 1900, so it is too narrow. The paragraph does not compare the speed of cars and horses. The idea about thousands of years is far too broad for a paragraph about city streets from 1900 to 1930.

4. C. The correct answer is C (Marcus slowly getting better at the trumpet by practising each evening): the passage follows Marcus from squeaky notes to playing a whole song after a month of practice. His sister’s reactions only show how much Marcus improved, so she is not the focus. The passage does not give advice about when to practice. The squeaks show that Marcus was a beginner, but the passage does not explain why the trumpet is hard.

5. A. The correct answer is A (How Sea Otters Use Kelp to Stay in Place While They Sleep): a good title covers the whole paragraph, and each sentence explains how kelp keeps sleeping otters from drifting. The sea floor is mentioned only because kelp is anchored there, so it is not the subject. The paragraph says kelp is a seaweed but does not describe what it is made of or how it grows. Swimming speed is not discussed in the paragraph.

6. A. The correct answer is A (Many plants and animals have their own ways of surviving in the desert): the first sentence says deserts are full of life, and each example shows a living thing surviving there. The cactus is one example, so it is too narrow to be the main idea. The paragraph does not compare deserts with other places. The night animals are one example among several, so they do not cover the whole paragraph.

7. D. The correct answer is D (how closing Elm Street to cars has changed Saturdays in Millbrook): each detail describes what happens on Elm Street now that cars are kept out on Saturdays. The band is one detail, and its songs are not described. The paragraph does not compare bikes with cars or say which is better. The vote is mentioned once, but the paragraph does not explain how the town votes.

8. D. The correct answer is D (The telephone made it much faster and easier for people to share news): the paragraph builds from slow letters to the telegraph to the telephone, showing how sharing news sped up. The time a letter took is one supporting detail, so it is too narrow. The paragraph does not say that telegraph offices were crowded. Phone use today is not discussed in the paragraph.

9. A. The correct answer is A (The old recipe notebook is a family treasure that brings everyone together): the old handwriting and the holiday cooking together show why the notebook matters to the family. The paragraph does not say that the grandmother worked as a cook. The stained pages are one detail, so they are too narrow. Calling holidays the best time to cook is an opinion the paragraph does not state.

10. A. The correct answer is A (the important work that volunteers do to help the animal shelter run): every sentence describes what volunteers do or how much they help. The paragraph does not give the number of animals at the shelter. Adoption is not mentioned in the paragraph. The paid workers are mentioned only to show why volunteers are needed.

11. B. The correct answer is B (Some giant sequoias grow taller than a building with 25 stories): this choice describes only one detail, the height of the trees, so it cannot cover the whole paragraph. The idea that sequoias are among the largest living things is the topic sentence, so it states the main idea. The choice about both height and width covers every detail in the paragraph. The choice about enormous size in many ways also sums up the whole paragraph.

12. C. The correct answer is C (Neighbours brought an old, unused mill back to life as a working museum): no single sentence states the main idea, but together the details show neighbours turning a silent mill into a working museum. The forty silent years are one detail about the past, so they are too narrow. The children watching the wheel are one detail about the present, so they are too narrow. The idea about old buildings is far too broad, and it is an opinion the paragraph does not give.

13. A. The correct answer is A (A thunderstorm can be dangerous even if it is not raining where you are): the first sentence states the main point, and the next two sentences explain and support it. The sentence about lightning striking far away gives the reason behind the main point, so it is a supporting detail. The advice to go indoors depends on what came before, as the words for this reason show. The main idea is stated directly in the first sentence, so it is not implied.
