Part A: Short Passages

101. B. The correct answer is B (An informational passage that explains facts about how glaciers form and move): the paragraph gives facts and explains a process, which is the job of informational writing. There are no characters or events, so the paragraph does not tell a story. The paragraph does not ask readers to do anything or try to change their minds. The language is plain and factual rather than creative or poetic.

102. C. The correct answer is C (It focuses on a character, her feelings and a moment in a story): the paragraph centres on Maya, her nervous feelings and what she sees, which is how stories work. The paragraph does not include any dates or numbers. The paragraph has no headings at all. The curtain rises in the story, but the paragraph does not explain how theaters do it.

103. B. The correct answer is B (A fable, which is a short story with animal characters that teaches a lesson): talking animals and a clear lesson about flattery are the marks of a fable. A news article reports real events, and real foxes do not speak to crows. The paragraph is not teaching facts about animal diets. The paragraph does not give any steps or instructions.

104. D. The correct answer is D (What helped Chicago grow so quickly in the years after 1850?): informational passages lead to questions about facts and causes, and the railroads are given as the cause of growth. The passage has no main character, so a question about feelings would not fit. The passage does not include a fox, a crow or a lesson. The passage is not a poem and does not try to create a mood.

105. B. The correct answer is B (To share a gentle story about a cat and the world she watches): the paragraph describes a character and her habits in a creative way, which is the job of a story. Fishing boats appear in the story, but the paragraph gives no facts about building them. The paragraph does not argue that cats are the best pets. No rules for lighthouse keepers are described.

106. D. The correct answer is D (Nonfiction, because it gives facts about real objects and real events): the paragraph gives facts about real cans and real energy savings, which is nonfiction. Nothing in the paragraph is imagined, so it is not fiction. A fable teaches a lesson through a short story, and this paragraph tells no story. The paragraph has no rhythm or rhyme.

107. D. The correct answer is D (The opening line is spoken by a character who calls to a friend): dialogue between characters is a strong signal of a literary passage. The paragraph does not give the height of the hills. The paragraph describes a storm but does not explain how storms form. There is no chart in the paragraph.

108. C. The correct answer is C (Facts, causes and effects about how dolphins are able to sleep): informational passages lead to questions about facts and causes, and this paragraph explains how dolphins sleep. The dolphin is not a character with feelings in a story. The paragraph teaches facts rather than a lesson about life. The words are plain and factual, not spooky or dark.

109. A. The correct answer is A (Fiction, because it is a made-up story with an imaginary character): a troll who collects buttons comes from the writer’s imagination, so the passage is fiction. Trolls are not real, so the passage is not reporting facts. Sorting buttons is one detail of the story, not a lesson in how to do it. The paragraph does not urge readers to do anything.

110. C. The correct answer is C (It is organized into clear sections that each explain a different topic): clear organization into sections that teach different topics is a mark of informational writing. The guide does not follow a character on adventures. There is no dialogue between campers in the description. The guide is not a story, so it has no twist ending.

111. D. Checking the clock could suggest boredom, but forgetting his line and rushing do not fit it. Nothing in the paragraph suggests that Sam is angry with the teacher. A proud, confident speaker would not stumble and forget his opening.

The change goes the other direction, from quiet to confident. She ate alone at the start of the year, not at the end. She was quiet at the start, and arguing came later.
