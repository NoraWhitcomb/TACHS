Explanations

201. A. The correct answer is A (My friends and I really liked the new reading room): the subject pronoun I belongs in the subject, and naming yourself last is the polite Standard English order. The version that begins with me uses an object pronoun as part of the subject, which is informal. Placing me after my friends still uses the object form where a subject pronoun is needed. The word myself should refer back to someone already named, so it cannot begin the subject this way.

202. A. The correct answer is A (The team does not have a chance of winning the trophy this year): it replaces the informal ain’t got with does not have and uses only one negative word. Pairing doesn’t with no creates a double negative, which Standard English avoids. The verb don’t is a plural form, but the team is singular here and needs doesn’t. The word ain’t is informal, so this version still does not meet the rules of Standard English.

203. A. The correct answer is A (A sentence can sound natural in speech and still break a grammar rule): the teacher is warning that the ear alone is not a reliable guide, because common errors can sound normal. The idea that natural-sounding speech is correct in writing is exactly the belief the teacher is correcting. Grammar rules apply to short sentences as well as long essays. Formal writing follows agreed rules, so it does not simply copy casual conversation.

204. D. The correct answer is D (We greatly enjoyed your dinosaur exhibit and learned a great deal from it): it keeps the thank-you message but uses formal, complete wording suited to a letter. The greeting hey and the phrase super cool are casual expressions that do not belong in a formal letter. The filler word like and the phrase coolest thing are informal speech habits. The words awesome and and stuff are informal, so this version still sounds like casual talk.

205. B. The correct answer is B (Writing a report that she will hand in to her science teacher): schoolwork such as a science report is formal writing, which follows the rules of Standard English. A quick text to a family member is a relaxed setting where informal English is common. A private diary is personal writing, so formal rules are not expected there. A lunch-table conversation with friends is casual speech rather than formal writing.

206. C. The correct answer is C (Check each sentence against a grammar rule that he already knows well): the chapter recommends listening first and then checking each sentence against a known rule. Reading faster makes it harder to catch mistakes, not easier. Asking a friend relies on the ear again, which is the method Omar already used. Sentence length is not the same as correctness, so splitting long sentences would not catch grammar errors.

207. B. The correct answer is B (Quietly, because it tells how the puppy waited by the door): an adverb describes a verb, and quietly tells how the puppy waited. The word nervous describes the noun puppy, which makes it an adjective. The word waited is the action itself, so it is a verb. The word by shows how the puppy relates to the door, which makes it a preposition.

208. A. The correct answer is A (Kindness, neighbours and city, because each names an idea, people or a place): kindness is an idea, neighbours are people and city is a place, so all three are nouns. The words helped and feel are verbs, and welcome describes how Rosa feels. The word her is a pronoun, while from and in are prepositions. The list mixes a noun with the verb helped and the pronoun her.

209. C. The correct answer is C (It, because it takes the place of the noun book in the sentence): the word it stands in for the book so that the noun does not have to be repeated. The word Maya is a proper noun, since it names a specific person. The word grabbed shows an action, so it is a verb. The word when joins the opening words to the rest of the sentence rather than replacing a noun.

The word loud is an adjective, and the is a small word that points to a noun.

Describing a noun is the job of an adjective, not a conjunction. Showing an action is the job of a verb, such as wanted. Naming a person, place or thing is the job of a noun.
