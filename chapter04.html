Explanations

Part A: Short Passages

281. C. The correct answer is C (Lila ran to the bus stop with her new backpack): it has a subject, Lila, a verb, ran, and it expresses a complete thought. The group of words that begins with ran has no subject, so the reader cannot tell who ran. The group of words about the tall boy has a subject but no verb telling what he did. The group of words that begins with because leaves the reader waiting for the rest of the thought.

282. C. The correct answer is C (Because the game was cancelled, we went to the movies instead): adding a main idea after the comma completes the thought that because begins. Adding more details still leaves the reader waiting to learn what happened because of the cancellation. Joining another reason with and still leaves no main idea, so the thought is incomplete. This version breaks the words apart and has no main verb at all.

283. B. The correct answer is B (We stayed inside because it was snowing hard that afternoon): joining the because part to the sentence before it creates one complete sentence. Keeping the period leaves the because part as a fragment on its own. Joining two complete thoughts with only a comma creates a comma splice. This version turns both parts into fragments, because neither has a subject and a complete verb.

284. B. The correct answer is B (A verb that tells what the students in the science club did): the caption names the students but never says what they did, so it needs a verb. The caption already has a subject, the students in the science club. The caption already begins with a capital letter. The caption already ends with a period, which is why it looks like a sentence.

285. B. The correct answer is B (Although many families in our town recycle paper and plastic each week): the word although begins a thought that is never finished, so the reader is left waiting. The sentence about families recycling has a subject, a verb and a complete thought. The sentence about the town’s collection day is also complete. The sentence about keeping plastic out of rivers has a subject, a verb and a complete thought.

286. D. The correct answer is D (We walked quietly, hoping to see a deer in the woods behind the cabin): adding a subject and a verb, we walked, gives the sentence someone who is hoping. Adding more description still leaves out the person who was hoping. Adding a comma after hoping does not supply a subject or a main verb. Adding commas around the place words still leaves the group without a subject.

287. D. The correct answer is D (The puppy slept soundly under the warm kitchen table): it has a subject, the puppy, a verb, slept, and it makes sense on its own. The group that begins with under has no subject and no verb. The group about the sleepy puppy has a subject but no verb. The group that begins with since leaves the reader waiting for the main idea.

288. B. The correct answer is B (The museum has a huge whale skeleton hanging from the ceiling in the main hall): joining the describing words to the sentence they describe creates one complete sentence. The words it hanging are not a complete verb, so the second part is still a fragment. Joining two complete thoughts with only a comma creates a comma splice. Adding a comma does not fix the second part, which is still a fragment.

289. B. The correct answer is B (Does this sentence leave me asking who, did what or what happened then?): a sentence that leaves those questions unanswered is missing a subject, a verb or a complete thought. Length does not decide whether a sentence is complete, since short sentences can be complete. Repeating a starting word may be a style issue, but it does not show whether a sentence is complete. Easy words can still form a fragment, so reading level is not the test.

290. A. The correct answer is A (It is a complete sentence, because the when part is joined to a main idea): the dependent clause beginning with when is followed by a complete main idea, the children ran outside. A sentence can begin with when and still be complete if a main idea follows. The subject, the children, tells who ran outside. The first part cannot stand alone, so this is not two complete sentences joined by a comma.

291. C. The correct answer is C (The bell rang, and the students hurried to the buses): adding a comma and the conjunction and joins the two complete sentences correctly. Placing the comma after students splits the second sentence in the wrong place. Adding only a comma turns the fused sentence into a comma splice. Placing the comma after and puts it on the wrong side of the conjunction.

292. C. The correct answer is C (Our team practiced that week. We won the final game on Saturday): splitting the run-on into two sentences puts a period where the first complete thought ends. Leaving the words together keeps the fused sentence. Adding only a comma creates a comma splice. Placing the comma after practiced changes the meaning and leaves the run-on.

293. B. The correct answer is B (The library was closed; we read on the front steps instead): the semicolon sits between two complete sentences that are closely related. A semicolon is not usually followed by and when it joins two simple sentences. Placing the semicolon after library splits the subject from its verb. This version keeps the comma splice and adds a semicolon in the wrong place.

294. C. The correct answer is C (When the power went out, we lit candles in the kitchen): adding when turns the first part into a dependent clause joined to the main idea. The word then is not a conjunction, so a comma before it still leaves a comma splice. Adding the word quickly does not change the comma splice. Removing the comma creates a fused sentence instead.

295. B. The correct answer is B (I ran I fell on the icy sidewalk outside the school): it joins two complete thoughts, I ran and I fell, with no punctuation or joining word. The sentence about the teachers is long, but its opening clause is correctly joined to the main idea. The sentence about the museum adds describing words between commas, which is correct. The sentence about the zoo joins two complete thoughts with a comma and a conjunction.

296. A. The correct answer is A (It is a comma splice, because two complete sentences are joined by only a comma): the fair was crowded and my friends and I waited are both complete sentences joined by only a comma. The sentence has verbs in both parts, was and waited. A comma alone is not enough to join two complete sentences. The word crowded correctly describes the fair.

297. D. The correct answer is D (At the word she, where a new subject and a new verb begin): she sells is a new subject and verb, so the second complete thought begins there. The word loves is the verb of the first complete thought. The word pictures is part of the second thought, not where it begins. The word market comes at the end of the second thought.

298. C. The correct answer is C (It started to rain, but we stayed at the park): but shows the contrast between rain starting and choosing to stay. The word so suggests that the rain caused them to stay, which does not make sense. The word because suggests that staying caused the rain, which is backward. Removing the comma creates a fused sentence.

299. D. The correct answer is D (The snow was deep, however the school stayed open that day): however is not a conjunction, so a comma before it still leaves a comma splice. Splitting the ideas into two sentences and starting the second with however is correct. Adding a comma and the conjunction but is a correct fix. Making the first part depend on the second with although is a correct fix.

300. D. The correct answer is D (The rain stopped, and a bright rainbow appeared over the hills): a comma and the conjunction and correctly join the two complete thoughts. Joining the two thoughts with nothing creates a fused sentence. Joining the two thoughts with only a comma creates a comma splice. Placing the comma after and puts it on the wrong side of the conjunction.

301. D. The correct answer is D (I was hungry, so I ate a sandwich before practice): so shows that eating the sandwich was a result of being hungry. The word but shows a contrast, and the two ideas do not contrast. The word or suggests a choice between the two ideas, which does not fit. The word although also shows a contrast, which does not fit.

302. B. The correct answer is B (Although the trail was steep, we reached the top before noon): although shows a contrast, which fits reaching the top quickly despite the steep trail. The word because suggests that the steepness helped them reach the top. The word so suggests that the steepness caused the early arrival. The word since also suggests a cause rather than a contrast.

303. C. The correct answer is C (The school play is on Friday, and it starts at seven o’clock): and adds a related fact, and the sentence keeps both pieces of information. The word but suggests a contrast that is not there. The word because suggests that the starting time causes the Friday date. The phrase which is a play repeats an idea and adds unneeded words.

304. C. The correct answer is C (After the movie ended, we walked to the diner for dessert): after the movie ended is a dependent clause joined to a complete main idea. The version with and joins two complete thoughts, which makes it compound. The version with a semicolon joins two complete thoughts, which makes it compound. The version with a period is two separate simple sentences.

305. C. The correct answer is C (Because the roads were icy, school opened two hours late): because shows that the icy roads caused the late opening. The word although shows a contrast, which does not fit a cause and its result. The word but also shows a contrast. The word or suggests a choice between two possibilities.

306. D. The correct answer is D (My cousin plays the cello and practices each evening after dinner): it joins both facts with and and does not add or remove anything. The city orchestra is a new fact that was not in the original sentences. Loving music more than sports is a new fact, and the time after dinner is missing. The piano is a new fact that was not in the original sentences.

307. C. The correct answer is C (While Dad cooked dinner, because it cannot stand alone as a sentence): the clause beginning with while has a subject and a verb but does not express a complete thought on its own. The words I set the table can stand alone, so they form the independent clause. The words Dad cooked are part of the dependent clause but do not include the word while that makes it dependent. The words the table are not a clause, because they have no subject or verb.

308. A. The correct answer is A (Sam missed the bus, so he was late for school): so shows the result clearly with no extra words. The words and so as a result repeat the same idea and make the sentence wordy. Joining two complete sentences with only a comma creates a comma splice. The word but suggests a contrast that is not there.

309. A. The correct answer is A (After we finished our homework, we went outside to play basketball): after shows that playing basketball came next in time. The word although shows a contrast, which does not fit two events in order. The version with because reverses the order and suggests the wrong cause. The word but also shows a contrast that is not there.

310. C. The correct answer is C (If, because it links the dependent clause to the main idea): the word if begins the dependent clause and connects it to the main idea about the picnic. The word rains is a verb, not a joining word. The word picnic is a noun that serves as the subject of the main clause. The word gym is a noun that names a place.

311. B. The correct answer is B (Because it was raining, we stayed home that afternoon): because says the same thing as due to the fact that in a single word. Keeping due to the fact that leaves the wordy phrase in place. The phrase owing to the reason that is just as wordy. The phrase because of the fact that still uses more words than needed.

312. A. The correct answer is A (The outcome of the game was a big surprise to the fans): outcome already means the result, so removing final takes out the repeated idea. Keeping final outcome leaves the redundancy in place. The phrase final end result repeats the same idea three times. The phrase outcome result repeats the idea of a result twice.

313. B. The correct answer is B (Each customer will receive a gift with a purchase this week): a gift is already free, so leaving out the word free avoids redundancy. The phrase free gift repeats an idea, since gifts are free. The phrase return back repeats the idea of going back. The phrase past history repeats an idea, since history is already in the past.

314. B. The correct answer is B (She served the children sandwiches on paper plates): placing on paper plates right after sandwiches shows what was on the plates. Keeping the original order still makes the children seem to be on the plates. Ending with the word to leaves a broken phrase. The words the children to sandwiches reverse the meaning and make the sentence confusing.

315. C. The correct answer is C (Running down the street, I lost my hat in the wind): the person running, I, now comes right after the describing phrase. The hat still comes right after the phrase, so it still seems to be running. The wind now comes right after the phrase, so it seems to be running. Changing the word order still does not name the person who was running.

316. A. The correct answer is A (Maya likes swimming, reading and biking on summer weekends): swimming, reading and biking all use the same -ing form. The words to read break the pattern of the -ing words. This version mixes to swim and to bike with reading. The words she reads break the pattern and create a confusing sentence.

317. D. The correct answer is D (The movie was exciting, funny and full of surprising twists): exciting, funny and surprising twists give the reader a clear idea of what the movie was like. The words really good and very nice are still vague. Repeating good does not tell the reader anything new. The words fine and pretty good are vague and add little meaning.

318. C. The correct answer is C (The library will close early on Friday for a staff meeting): it gives the day, the change and the reason in the fewest words. The phrase be closing earlier than usual uses more words to say close early. The phrase for the reason of is wordier than for. The phrase on the day of Friday adds words without adding meaning.

319. D. The correct answer is D (Driving home from the lake, we saw a deer near the road): placing driving home from the lake next to we shows who was driving. Keeping the phrase after deer still makes the deer seem to be driving. This version clearly says the deer was driving. Moving the phrase into the middle makes the sentence confusing.

320. D. The correct answer is D (Members can learn chess quickly and easily at our weekly club): quickly and easily are both adverbs, so the pair is parallel. The phrase with ease does not match the single adverb quickly. The phrase in a quick way does not match the adverb easily. The phrase in an easy manner does not match the adverb quickly.

321. D. The correct answer is D (I think the new park is a great place for families): I think already shows that this is the writer’s opinion, so in my opinion is not needed. Keeping both phrases repeats the same idea. The words own personal opinion repeat the idea of personal three times. Placing in my opinion in the middle still repeats the same idea.

322. B. The correct answer is B (At the farm, we fed the goats, milked a cow and collected eggs): fed, milked and collected all use the same past tense form, and the sentence names who did the actions. The word milking breaks the pattern of the past tense verbs. This version has a dangling modifier, because the eggs seem to be feeding the goats. The phrase the collection of eggs does not match the pattern of the two verbs.

323. D. The correct answer is D (The team celebrated its victory with a pizza party): it keeps the full meaning in the fewest words. The phrase by having a party where pizza was served is wordier than with a pizza party. The phrases after it won and the victory it had repeat the same idea. The phrase had a celebration of its victory that included a party uses many extra words.

324. D. The correct answer is D (The main character is brave, kind and loyal to her friends): the sentence uses one subject, the main character, with the verb is. Adding she after the main character gives the verb two subjects. Removing the comma still leaves two subjects for one verb. This version repeats she and makes the sentence even more confusing.

325. A. The correct answer is A (Sentence 3, because it is about the writer’s cereal, not about how bees make honey): the paragraph explains how bees make honey, and a favourite cereal is a different subject. Sentence 1 is the topic sentence, so it belongs at the start. Sentence 2 gives a supporting detail about the process. Sentence 4 also gives a supporting detail about the process.

326. A. The correct answer is A (Owls have several special features that help them hunt at night): each detail describes a feature that helps owls hunt, so this sentence covers the whole paragraph. A personal favourite does not state the main idea of the details. Where owls live is not discussed in the paragraph. Owls’ food is not the focus of the three details.

327. D. The correct answer is D (Sentence 2, then sentence 1, then sentence 4, then sentence 3): the signal words first, then, next and finally show the correct order of the steps. This order starts with then, which cannot come before first. This order puts finally before then, which breaks the sequence. This order starts with next, which cannot come before first.

328. C. The correct answer is C (The word this refers to the faster trips described in sentence 2): a sentence that refers back with this must come after the sentence that explains what this means. The topic sentence about the bridge comes first, and sentence 3 is a result, not the topic. Sentence length does not decide the order of ideas. Sentence 3 does not name a year.

329. B. The correct answer is B (Describing a tree house from its roof down to the ladder on the ground): moving from the roof down to the ladder describes the tree house by location. Telling events from first to last uses time order. Explaining why something happened and what resulted uses cause and effect order. Listing reasons from least to most important uses order of importance.

330. C. The correct answer is C (Cause and effect order, because it explains a storm and its results): the storm is the cause, and the lost power and closed schools are the effects. The paragraph does not describe places by location. The paragraph does not rank ideas from least to most important. The paragraph does not list steps for fixing anything.

331. A. The correct answer is A (With a few simple changes, our lunches could be both healthy and tasty): a concluding sentence wraps up the idea, and this one sums up the point about improving lunches. Her brother’s school is a new subject that wanders off topic. The number of tables is a new detail rather than a conclusion. Bringing lunch from home is another detail, not a way of wrapping up.

332. D. The correct answer is D (Between sentences 1 and 2, because collecting the sap comes before boiling it): the sap must be collected before it can be boiled, so sentence 3 fits between the other two. Sentence 1 is the topic sentence, so it should stay first. Collecting the sap happens before boiling, so it is not the last step. Collecting sap is an important step in making syrup, so it belongs in the paragraph.

333. D. The correct answer is D (Helmets can prevent serious head injuries in a bad crash): preventing serious injuries is the most important reason, so it belongs last. Bright colours are a minor reason compared with safety. Keeping sun out of the eyes is a small benefit. Being light and easy to carry is less important than preventing injury.

334. B. The correct answer is B (Read the paragraph with the sentence in each spot and see where it flows best): testing each spot shows where the sentence connects naturally with the sentences around it. The end is not always the right place, because the sentence may introduce or explain an idea. Space on the page has nothing to do with the logic of the paragraph. Sentence length does not show where an idea belongs.

335. C. The correct answer is C (However, which shows a contrast between the two ideas): the second sentence presents a different side, so a contrast word fits. The words in addition suggest that the ideas agree, which does not fit. Finding science difficult is not an example of enjoying it. Enjoying science does not cause some students to find it difficult.

336. A. The correct answer is A (As a result, which shows that the closed bridge caused the detour): the detour happened because the bridge was closed, so a cause and effect transition fits. Taking a longer road does not contrast with the bridge being closed. The detour is a result of the closing, not an example of it. The writer is not summing up a whole topic.

337. B. The correct answer is B (For example, which introduces a specific case of the first idea): swimming lessons are one example of the ways to stay active. The swimming lessons are not surprising given the first sentence. The swimming lessons do not replace the first idea. The sentences are not steps in a process.

338. A. The correct answer is A (Next, which shows the following step in the directions): pressing the seeds comes after filling the pot, so next shows the following step. Pressing seeds does not contrast with filling the pot. The directions are not ending after only two steps. Pressing seeds is not an example of filling the pot.

339. D. The correct answer is D (In addition, which adds another benefit to the first idea): both sentences give benefits, so a transition that adds an idea fits. The second sentence agrees with the first rather than opposing it. Saving energy does not cause less trash, so a result word does not fit. Reducing trash is not an example of saving energy.

340. A. The correct answer is A (It was too cold to play outside. Therefore, we played board games instead): a comma follows therefore when it starts the sentence. Placing the comma after we splits the subject from its verb. Joining the two sentences with commas around therefore creates a comma splice. A semicolon is not used after a transition at the start of a sentence.

341. B. The correct answer is B (Contrast, so the best transition here would be nevertheless): winning the championship is surprising after three losses, so a contrast word such as nevertheless fits. Winning the championship is not an example of losing games. The second idea does not add a similar point to the first. The word first would suggest the start of a list, which does not fit the end of the season.

342. A. The correct answer is A (In summary, which shows that the writer is wrapping up the essay): the sentence sums up the main points, so a transition that sums up fits. The sentence restates the main points rather than giving one example. The sentence agrees with the essay rather than disagreeing. The essay is ending, not moving to another step.

343. A. The correct answer is A (Instead, which shows that walking replaced the original plan): Kai walked in place of riding, so instead fits. Walking was not done in addition to riding. The sentences show a change of plan, not a similarity. Planning to ride did not cause the walk; the flat tire did.

344. C. The correct answer is C (Adding transitions that show how each idea connects to the next): transitions guide readers by showing how one idea connects to the next. Longer sentences with extra words can make writing less clear. Starting each sentence the same way makes writing repetitive rather than connected. Removing the topic sentence makes the paragraph harder to follow.

345. C. The correct answer is C (Many errors show up only when she sees how the parts fit together): an error such as a verb that does not match an earlier subject can only be spotted by reading everything. The underlined part is often where the error is, so it should not be skipped. Reading more carefully takes extra time rather than saving it. No rule says the answer is found in the first word.

346. A. The correct answer is A (It is not the best answer, because it fixes one error but adds another): a good answer fixes the problem without creating a new one, and this choice creates a comma splice. Fixing the verb is not enough if the choice adds a new error. A comma alone cannot join two complete sentences. Length alone does not decide whether a choice is correct.

347. D. The correct answer is D (The sentence is correct as it is written and needs no change): the plural subject students agrees with practice, and the sentence has no other errors. The verb practices is singular, so it does not agree with students. The comma separates the subject from its verb for no reason. This version changes the tense and adds a comma splice.

348. A. The correct answer is A (A fixed order stops her from skipping a step when she feels rushed): following the same order each time makes sure she checks each type of error. The checklist does not change the length of the test questions. The checklist is about finding errors, not about guessing answer letters. The point of the list is to check each step, not to skip any.

357. B. The correct answer is B (Riders who fell from these tall bicycles were often badly hurt): it tells exactly who was hurt and how seriously, which gives a clearer picture. 359. D. The correct answer is D (Because the challenge was such a success, the principal made an announcement): adding a main idea after the comma completes the thought that because begins.
