Explanations

737. D. The correct answer is D (Rosa plays in the school band at Lakeview School this year): the first premise says each chess club member plays in the band, and the second premise places Rosa in the chess club. The idea that Rosa joined because she enjoys music adds a reason the premises never give. The claim that each band student is in the chess club reverses the first premise, which only says chess club members play in the band. The premises never compare Rosa’s chess skill with the skill of other club members.

738. A. The correct answer is A (Yesterday was a weekday, because the bakery sells bread only on weekdays): the bakery sells bread only on weekdays, so the day Mr. Ortiz bought bread there must have been a weekday. A single purchase yesterday does not show that Mr. Ortiz has a weekly habit. The premises prove that yesterday was a weekday, but they do not show which weekday it was. The bakery might open on weekends to sell other goods, because the premises only limit the days it sells bread.

739. C. The correct answer is C (Bus 42 will reach the library before the library opens at nine): the premises say nothing about when Bus 42 arrives, so its arrival time cannot be compared with the opening time. The statement that Bus 42 stops at the library is proved true, because each Route 9 bus stops there and Bus 42 is on Route 9. The statement that Bus 42 skips the library is proved false by the same two premises. The opening time is stated directly in the third premise, so that statement is true.

740. B. The correct answer is B (The tallest plant in the Zorn museum garden has blue leaves): the premises say each tree on Zorn has blue leaves, and the tallest plant in the garden is a tree. The idea of green leaves comes from real trees on Earth rather than from the premises, which must be treated as true. The premises describe trees only, so they say nothing about the leaves of other plants in the garden. The premises never say who planted the tree near the gate.

741. C. The correct answer is C (Lena is older than her cousin Ivy, who is younger than Sam): Lena is older than Sam and Sam is older than Ivy, so Lena must be older than Ivy. The premises place Theo below Lena, but they never compare Theo with Sam. Sam cannot be the youngest, because the premises say Sam is older than Ivy. The premises never compare the ages of Ivy and Theo.

742. C. The correct answer is C (It is impossible to tell whether Nadia plays for the Hawks soccer team): the premise tells us that Hawks players wear red, not that people who wear red are Hawks players. Wearing a red jersey does not make Nadia a Hawk, because the premise only says that Hawks players wear red. Being at the park does not rule out playing for the team, since the premises say nothing about where Hawks players spend Saturdays. The premises never mention a game at the park on Saturday.

743. C. The correct answer is C (The science fair at Hill School takes place in March this year): the fair is held only in the gym, and the gym is closed in March, so the fair cannot take place in March. The statement that the fair is in the gym is proved true, because the fair is held only in the gym. An April fair is possible, but the premises do not prove the month, so that statement is impossible to tell. The premises say the gym is closed in March but never say when it reopens.

744. D. The correct answer is D (Marcus may choose a book from the prize shelf this week): each gold star student may choose a prize book, and Marcus earned a gold star this week. The premise says students may choose a book, not that they will, and it never says which kind Marcus prefers. The premises mention Marcus but say nothing about how many other students earned stars. The premises never say that Marcus has chosen a book yet.

745. B. The correct answer is B (The Garcia family owns exactly one pet in addition to Pepper): the family owns two pets and Pepper is one of them, so there is exactly one other pet. The premises never say what kind of animal the second pet is. The premises give Pepper’s age but not the age of the second pet. The premises never say how or when the family got Pepper.

746. A. The correct answer is A (The students lined up at the door after the bell rang today): the condition happened, because the bell rang at three o’clock, so the result follows and the students lined up. The statement says the bell leads to lining up, so it does not make lining up the cause of the bell. The statement says nothing about the students leaving the building. The premises describe what happened today, not the bell schedule for other days.

747. B. The correct answer is B (Ellie did not practice the piano on Tuesday): the result did not happen, so by the contrapositive the condition did not happen either. Practising without playing a song would break the true statement, which says practice leads to a song. The premises never mention a visit, so this adds a reason that was not given. The premises say nothing about Ellie playing for a friend.

748. D. The correct answer is D (It is impossible to tell whether Jamal’s sour fruit was a lemon): the statement says lemons taste sour, but other fruits may also taste sour, so a sour taste does not prove the fruit was a lemon. Deciding the fruit was a lemon because it tasted sour uses the converse, which the statement does not prove. The idea about how lemons are eaten comes from outside the premises. The premises never mention limes, so naming one adds a fact that was not given.

749. C. The correct answer is C (It is impossible to tell whether school started late this morning): the statement only tells what happens when it snows, so on a night without snow school might start late for another reason or start on time. Deciding that school started on time falls into the trap of assuming no snow means no late start. The premises never link icy roads to a late start, so this adds a rule that was not given. The premises never mention school being closed.

750. B. The correct answer is B (The school holds a rally, because the team plays in the final): the Friday win leads to playing in the final, and playing in the final leads to a rally, so the chain ends with a rally. The statements say the team plays in the final, but they never say whether it wins that game. The chain runs from the Friday win to the rally, so the rally cannot be the cause of the win. The statements make the rally depend on playing in the final, not on winning it.

751. C. The correct answer is C (Mia was allowed to go to the movies after cleaning her room): cleaning her room means Mia can go to the movies, which is permission rather than proof that she went. The popcorn depends on Mia actually going to the movies, and the premises only say that she can go. Cleaning her room gave Mia permission to go, but the premises do not prove that she went. The premises never give Mia’s reason for cleaning her room.

752. B. The correct answer is B (An animal that does not have eight legs is not a spider): this is the contrapositive, which moves backward along the arrow by making both parts negative. Saying an eight-legged animal must be a spider moves backward along the arrow without making the parts negative, which is the converse. Saying a non-spider lacks eight legs is the trap of assuming that no condition means no result. The statement covers spiders of each age, so adding a rule about adults goes beyond what was given.

753. D. The correct answer is D (If the front window lights are off, then the library is not open): it switches the two parts of the statement and makes both of them negative. Switching the parts without making them negative creates the converse, which is not the contrapositive. Making the parts negative without switching them creates a different statement, which the original does not prove. Keeping the condition and changing the result contradicts the original statement.

754. A. The correct answer is A (Oliver fed the fish this morning, because the fish swam to the top): it treats the fish swimming to the top as proof that Oliver fed them, which reverses the statement. The conclusion about fish that did not swim up is the contrapositive, which is a safe conclusion. The conclusion about tomorrow moves forward along the statement, which is also safe. The conclusion about Oliver not feeding the fish is the other trap, which assumes no condition means no result.

755. D. The correct answer is D (The dog might be barking for a reason other than the doorbell): the brother uses the converse, but barking does not prove the doorbell rang because the dog could bark for another reason. Saying the dog might not bark at the doorbell contradicts the statement, which is given as true. Whether someone is at the door has no effect on the logic of the brother’s conclusion. What the brother heard is beside the point, because barking does not prove the bell rang in either case.

756. D. The correct answer is D (True, because some means at least one, and possibly the whole class): some means at least one, and the whole class rode the bus, so at least one student did. In logic some does not suggest that a student was left out, so this mistakes the everyday meaning for the logic meaning. The premise already tells how many rode, so the statement can be decided. A statement can be true even when a stronger statement is also true.

757. B. The correct answer is B (Each painting in Room 3 is for sale at the spring fair): the Room 3 circle sits inside the Pine School circle, which sits inside the circle of things for sale. The claim that each fair item is a Room 3 painting reverses the statements, since the fair may sell other things. The statements prove that each Room 3 painting is for sale, so this contradicts them. The statements never say where or when the students made their paintings.

758. C. The correct answer is C (No turtle at the Riverside Zoo has fur on its body): the turtle circle sits inside the reptile circle, and the reptile circle does not touch the fur circle. Turtles with fur would be reptiles with fur, which the first statement rules out. The statements say reptiles lack fur, not that each animal without fur is a reptile. The statements never say how many other reptiles the zoo keeps.

759. D. The correct answer is D (It is not proved, because the trees with nests might not be oaks): the oak circle sits inside the tall tree circle, but the part with nests could sit outside the oak circle. Being among the tall trees does not place the oaks among the trees with nests. The statements say some tall trees have nests, so they do not place the nests in shorter trees. The tall trees with nests could be maples or pines, so the oaks might have no nests.

760. D. The correct answer is D (the diagram with the robins circle drawn inside the birds circle): each robin belongs to the group of birds, while many birds are not robins. The diagram with the robins circle and the birds circle apart, not touching shows that no robin is a bird. The diagram with the robins circle and the birds circle overlapping shows that some robins are not birds, which the statement rules out. The diagram with the birds circle drawn inside the robins circle shows that each bird is a robin, which reverses the statement.

761. C. The correct answer is C (the diagram with the fish circle and the mammals circle apart, not touching): no member of the fish group belongs to the mammal group, so the two circles must not touch. The diagram with the fish circle and the mammals circle overlapping shows that some fish are mammals. The diagram with the fish circle drawn inside the mammals circle shows that each fish is a mammal. The diagram with the mammals circle drawn inside the fish circle shows that each mammal is a fish.

762. B. The correct answer is B (Jo is a member of both the band and the school choir): the name sits in the part where the two circles overlap, which belongs to both groups. A name inside the band circle only would mean Jo is not in the choir, but the name sits where the circles overlap. A name inside the choir circle only would mean Jo is not in the band. A name outside both circles would mean Jo belongs to neither group.

763. D. The correct answer is D (Some students in Room 12 at Oak School are not shy): the Room 12 students on the debate team are inside the debate circle, which does not touch the shy circle. The Room 12 students who are not on the debate team could still be shy. Shy debate team members would contradict the first statement. The second statement says some Room 12 students are on the team, not each of them.

764. B. The correct answer is B (It is impossible to tell whether Dana is in the hiking club): the club circle sits inside the boot owner circle, so Dana could be inside the club circle or outside it. Owning boots does not place Dana in the club, because the club circle sits inside the boot owner circle. Many people owning boots does not rule out Dana being a member. The statements never say that Dana hikes with the club.

765. A. The correct answer is A (A book on the top shelf may be checked out of the library): each top shelf book is a dictionary, and no dictionary may be checked out. A dictionary on the bottom shelf is possible, because the statements do not say which books are on that shelf. This statement must be true, since the bottom shelf books that may be checked out cannot be dictionaries. The statements never compare the number of dictionaries and storybooks, so this could be true.

766. C. The correct answer is C (Omar): the order from first to last is Rafael, Omar, Pia and Quinn. Rafael finished first, ahead of Omar. Pia finished third, behind Omar. Quinn finished last, behind Pia.

768. D. The correct answer is D (No book sits between the atlas and the dictionary on the shelf): directly to the left means the atlas and dictionary are side by side with no book between them. Somewhere to the left allows other books to sit between the novel and the atlas. The atlas sits directly to the left of the dictionary, so the novel cannot take that place. The clues do not say whether other books sit to the right of the dictionary.

769. A. The correct answer is A (The green car is parked in either the second or fourth space): the red car is in space 3, so the spaces next to it are 2 and 4. 770. A. The correct answer is A (Box S is heavier than box R, which is the lightest box): the order from heaviest to lightest is S, P, Q and R. Box S is heavier than box P, so box P cannot be the heaviest. Box Q is heavier than box R and lighter than box S, which is the opposite of this statement. The clues say box R is lighter than box Q.

771. D. The correct answer is D (The florist): the gym, bakery, dentist and library take floors 1, 2, 3 and 5, which leaves floor 4. The dentist is on floor 3, directly above the bakery. The gym is on floor 1, the only floor below the bakery. The library is on floor 5, the top floor.

773. D. The correct answer is D (Di): Bo must take seat 4, because seat 3 would split Di and Cy, so Di sits in seat 2 and Cy in seat 3. Bo cannot sit in seat 2, because that seat is next to Ada. Cy sits in seat 3, directly to the right of Di. Ada sits in seat 1, according to the second clue.

774. A. 776. D. The correct answer is D (book): a petal is part of a flower, and a page is part of a book. Paper is what a page is made of, not the whole that a page is part of. Read is an action done with a page, not a whole. A library holds books, so it does not complete the part-to-whole sentence.

777. D. The correct answer is D (dig): a broom is used to sweep, and a shovel is used to dig. Dirt is what a shovel moves, not the action a shovel is used for. A garden is a place where a shovel may be used. A rake is another tool, not an action.

778. B. The correct answer is B (flower): an oak is a kind of tree, and a rose is a kind of flower. A thorn is part of a rose, not the group a rose belongs to. A garden is a place where roses grow. Red is a colour that some roses have.

779. B. The correct answer is B (end): begin and start are synonyms, and end is a synonym of finish. Start is an antonym of finish, but the first pair is made of synonyms. Line forms the phrase finish line rather than a word with the same meaning. Winning can happen at the finish, but win does not mean the same as finish.

780. A. The correct answer is A (wide): shallow is the opposite of deep, and narrow is the opposite of wide. Thin is close in meaning to narrow, so it forms a synonym pair. Long describes length, not the opposite of narrow. A river can be narrow, but river is not an opposite.

781. B. The correct answer is B (freezing): soaked is a much stronger form of damp, and freezing is a much stronger form of chilly. Cool is about as mild as chilly, so it does not show a stronger form. Warm is nearly the opposite of chilly. Winter is a season that may be chilly, not a stronger form of chilly.

782. C. The correct answer is C (library): a pilot works in a cockpit, and a librarian works in a library. A book is something a librarian works with, not the place where a librarian works. A reader is a person a librarian helps. An airport is linked to the pilot, not to the librarian.

783. A. The correct answer is A (pen): a painter uses a brush, and a writer uses a pen. A story is what a writer makes, not the tool used to make it. A desk is a place where a writer may work. An author is another word for a writer, not a tool.

784. C. The correct answer is C (cow): a kitten grows into a cat, and a calf grows into a cow. Milk comes from a cow, but it is not what a calf grows into. A farm is a place where calves live. A puppy is another young animal, not the adult form of a calf.

785. A. The correct answer is A (foot): a glove covers a hand, and a sock covers a foot. A shoe is worn with a sock, but it is clothing rather than the body part a sock covers. A toe is only part of what a sock covers. A sock covers the foot rather than the leg.

786. A. The correct answer is A (10): 3 × 4 = 12, and 12 − 2 = 10. Six comes from subtracting 2 first and then tripling, which reverses the order of the steps. Twelve triples 4 but leaves out the subtraction. Fourteen adds 2 instead of subtracting it.

787. C. The correct answer is C (26): 2▲ = (2 + 3) × 2 = 10, and 10▲ = (10 + 3) × 2 = 26. Ten is the value of 2▲ alone, before the rule is used a second time. Seventeen comes from doubling first and then adding 3 at each step. Twenty doubles 10 but leaves out adding 3 in the second step.

788. B. The correct answer is B (DKTF): B becomes D, I becomes K, R becomes T and D becomes F. CJSE moves each letter only one place forward. ZGPB moves each letter two places backward instead of forward. DKTE moves the last letter only one place, from D to E.

789. B. The correct answer is B (TEA): U becomes T, F becomes E and B becomes A when each letter moves one place back. VGC moves each letter forward again instead of backward. TEB moves the first two letters back but leaves the last letter unchanged. SDZ moves each letter two places backward.

791. A. The correct answer is A (18): 10 − 4 = 6, and 6 × 3 = 18. Twenty-six multiplies by 3 first and then subtracts 4, which reverses the steps. Six subtracts 4 but leaves out multiplying by 3. Thirty multiplies by 3 but leaves out subtracting 4.

793. B. The correct answer is B (6): C = 3, A = 1 and B = 2, and 3 + 1 + 2 = 6. Three hundred twelve writes the letter values side by side instead of adding them. Five adds C and B but leaves out the value of A. Three is the value of C alone.

794. A. The correct answer is A (10): 5 × 3 = 15, and 15 − 5 = 10. Twelve subtracts the second number, 3, instead of the first number. Fifteen multiplies the numbers but leaves out the subtraction. Two subtracts 3 from 5 without multiplying.
